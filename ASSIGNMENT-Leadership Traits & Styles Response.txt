Leadership Traits & Styles Response

Two leadership traits from Chapter 2 that I identify with the most are integrity and determination. Integrity is important to me because I believe leadership requires honesty and consistency, especially when making difficult decisions that affect others. Determination also stands out because leaders often face setbacks and criticism, and without persistence it is easy to give up. A leader from the chapter who reflects both of these traits is Abraham Lincoln. He demonstrated integrity by standing firm on his values during the Civil War, even when his decisions were unpopular, and he showed determination by continuing to lead the nation through extreme division and uncertainty. These traits helped him gain respect and trust from others over time.

Theory X and Theory Y represent two very different ways leaders view people at work. Theory X assumes that workers dislike responsibility, avoid work, and need to be controlled closely to perform well. Theory Y assumes that people are naturally motivated, capable of responsibility, and want to contribute when given the opportunity. I relate more to Theory Y because I believe most people want to do well when they feel respected and supported. Leaders who trust their employees and give them responsibility often create better teamwork and stronger motivation. This approach also encourages creativity and long-term commitment rather than fear or pressure.

My leadership style aligns most closely with the democratic style discussed in Chapter 3. I prefer this style because it allows others to share ideas and feel included in decisions, which helps build trust and cooperation. A leader who demonstrates this style is Franklin D. Roosevelt, who often relied on advisors and encouraged discussion during the Great Depression and World War II. His leadership helped people feel supported during difficult times and strengthened confidence in the government. Democratic leadership is effective because it balances guidance with participation, allowing leaders to remain in control while still valuing the voices of others.
