Understanding Leadership Response

After reading Chapter 1, I realized that I mostly see leadership as influence and also as a process. Leadership as influence makes sense to me because a leader does not need a title to influence others. In the chapter, it explains that leadership is about motivating and guiding people toward a common goal, not just giving orders. My Conceptualizing Leadership Questionnaire results showed that I value teamwork, communication, and motivating others, which connects closely with this view. I also see leadership as a process because leadership develops over time through relationships and shared experiences. This means anyone can grow into a leader by learning and interacting with others, not just people in high positions.

The chapter also explains that leadership and management are not the same thing. Management is more focused on planning, organizing, and making sure rules and tasks are followed. Leadership, on the other hand, is more about inspiring people and helping them move toward positive change. I have seen this difference in a workplace where a manager mostly focused on schedules, deadlines, and rules, but did not really connect with employees. At the same time, there was a leader who encouraged the team, listened to concerns, and motivated people to do their best. Even though both roles were important, the leader had a bigger impact on morale and motivation.

From the GLOBE studies in Chapter 1, the two leadership attributes I think are most important are integrity and inspirational motivation. Integrity is important because people need to trust their leaders. If a leader is dishonest or unfair, people will not want to follow them. Inspirational motivation is also important because leaders need to encourage others, especially during difficult times. Chapter 1 explains that leaders inspire confidence and support, which connects to both of these qualities. I believe leaders who act with integrity and inspire others are more successful in creating positive change.
