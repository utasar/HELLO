Mechanism:

Wireless fast phone charger (charging pad)



Technical Definition (classification + differentiation):

A wireless fast charger is a power-transfer device that supplies electrical energy to a smartphone by transmitting energy through an electromagnetic field between two flat, circular surfaces without requiring a physical electrical connector between the phone and the charger.



Major Parts (in order of description):

* Charging surface (top pad)

&nbsp;   The flat, circular upper surface where the smartphone is placed for charging.



* Indicator light ring

&nbsp;  The illuminated ring around the edge of the charger that displays charging status.



* Protective casing (body)

&nbsp;  The rigid circular shell that encloses and supports the internal components.



* Power input port and cable connection

&nbsp;  The port on the side of the charger where the USB power cable connects.



Relation to Computer Science:

This mechanism relates to computer science because it provides power to smartphones, which function as mobile computing systems. Applications, operating systems, networking processes, and data storage depend on consistent electrical power to operate correctly. Wireless charging systems also interact with device firmware and power-management software to control charging speed, regulate temperature, and protect battery health. Understanding how hardware devices supply energy to computing systems is important for designing reliable software and embedded systems.
